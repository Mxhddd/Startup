Safe school runs, driven by women

Pohnch Baji puts women behind the wheel of Karachi's school vans: daytime work on fixed routes with known families, run on the Pohnch app that parents already use to know where their children are.

The problem

  • Parents hand children to a private van and hear nothing until they are home. The worry is sharpest for girls.
  • Women's labour-force participation in Sindh was 11% in 2021.1 Safe, local, flexible paid work is scarce.
  • Women want a say in who drives them: among women who use ride-hailing, 33% wanted to choose their driver's gender, 30% had felt unsafe and 15% had been harassed.2

Why school runs fit

Daylight only: about 6:30–9:00 and 12:30–3:00. Same route, same children, same families every day. A mother can carry her own children with the others. Work ends before evening; no strangers, no night trips.

Three ways in

Woman driver: licensed, verified, trained. Mother-driver: drives her own and neighbours' children for a second household income. Woman attendant: no licence needed; rides with any driver and settles the children.

Built on Pohnch

Every Baji van runs the Pohnch app: the driver taps when the van leaves, when each child boards and when they reach school or home; parents get a phone alert for each tap. A parent "captain" approves every family phone, sees the driver's week, and gets an emergency alert with location the moment the driver presses for help.

Others have shown demand

  • Pink Taxi (Paxi), Karachi: women drivers for women passengers; one driver had driven a school van before.3
  • Sindh's women-only Pink Bus (2023) is training women to drive its buses.4
  • VSisters launched women-only ride-hailing in 2025; Careem has had more than 1,500 women captains.2
